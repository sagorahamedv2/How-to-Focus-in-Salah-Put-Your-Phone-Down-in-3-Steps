The adhan goes off. You tell yourself, “one minute.” You pick up your phone to check one thing. Twenty minutes later, you rush through your salah and can’t remember a word you said.

السلام عليكم ورحمة الله. Over the next four minutes, I’ll give you three small steps to put your phone down before salah, so you’re actually present when you say الله أكبر. No guilt. Just setup.

Here’s the reframe. Allah describes success like this:

قَدْ أَفْلَحَ الْمُؤْمِنُونَ ۝ الَّذِينَ هُمْ فِي صَلَاتِهِمْ خَاشِعُونَ

Successful indeed are the believers, those who are humble in their prayer. Quran, chapter 23, verses 1 and 2.

Khushu, humility and presence, is something we strive for. But most of us try to build it in the middle of salah, with a phone buzzing in the next room or the next pocket. Focus is mostly decided before you start. So don’t fight your phone with willpower. Remove the fight.

Three steps. One word each. Away. Silent. Replace.

Away. When the adhan calls حَيَّ عَلَى الصَّلَاةِ, come to prayer, put your phone in another room. Not face down on the mat. Another room. Tomorrow, pick one prayer and leave your phone in a bag or a drawer before you make wudu.

Picture someone at work at Dhuhr: phone in the desk drawer, or in a locker, before they walk to pray. If you have to stand up to get it, you’ll usually leave it.

Silent. Set your phone’s focus mode or Do Not Disturb to switch on by itself for about fifteen minutes around each prayer. Let through only calls from family or true emergencies. Now your phone makes the decision for you, so you don’t have to.

If you’re a student in the library, the group chat can wait ten minutes. It will still be there.

Replace. The urge to reach for your phone doesn’t just vanish. It needs somewhere to go. So in the minutes after the adhan, make wudu slowly. Sit. Breathe. Do whatever you normally do before you stand to pray. Scholars differ on what’s best in that window, so ask a qualified scholar if you’re unsure. The goal is simple: fill the gap with something other than a screen.

Here’s what Dhuhr might look like. Five minutes before, the phone goes in the bag. At the adhan, focus mode switches on. Then wudu, slowly. Then you pray. Use your own times; the shape is what matters.

The most common excuse? “I need my phone for prayer times.” Fair. So move that job somewhere else. Use a cheap alarm clock, a watch, or a printed timetable on the fridge. And if your phone has to be the alarm, silence it, walk away, and don’t open it.

You don’t need all three tomorrow. Pick one prayer, the one you rush the most, and do just the first step: Away. That’s it. Salah isn’t something we optimize. It’s the anchor the day is built around. We’re only clearing the path to it.
